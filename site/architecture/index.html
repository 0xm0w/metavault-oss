Architecture

A dumb vault. A warden that never sees the key.

The layer that holds secrets does not talk to agents. The layer that talks to agents does not hold secrets.

Three layers

Who is allowed to know what

App

Approval, vault, logs, policies

Plain TypeScript in Electron. It shows you the decision. It does not keep the secret.

vaultd

The dumb vault

AES-256-GCM, OS keychain, the policy floor, single-use grants. Keys are decrypted here for one call — never persisted, never logged.

Edges

Agents and the warden

IDEs speak MCP. The warden scores metadata. Neither side receives the key.

One request

Denied early, or returned without a key

A self-approve hits 401. MCP has no approve tool. The daemon token stays in the app and the CLI.

The floor

These do not depend on the model

No secret in the transcript

MCP, HTTP, logs, the UI, and the model prompt. A canary covers 27 surfaces and fails the build on a hit.

Plaintext stays in memory

Decrypted at call time for one grant. The app never writes it to disk and never logs it.

Narrow, never widen

The warden can shrink a scope. It cannot step past the alias ceiling.

Grants expire

After the call, after the TTL, or the moment you revoke.

Localhost only

The daemon does not listen on the network. Human routes want the daemon token.

Model down is still safe

Fooled, malformed, or missing: policy and you decide. The floor does not move.

Meta models

Judgement on top of the law

The shipped catalogue is what Settings exposes. A larger model is better at spotting a write phrased as a read. It is not a wider permission.

Like a wallet

Keys are used, not shown

Encrypted at rest. A human approves. The secret is not a field you can copy back out.

Unlike a signature

The payload is an API call

Who asked, which scope, what they claimed, and what the warden thinks. That context is the product.
